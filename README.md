## Abstact
Parasites impose a societal burden by affecting human and animal health. In animals, parasite community composition can directly influence the prevalence of (sub-)clinical disease, spread of resistance, and incurred monetary losses. Here, we evaluated previously sequenced field nematode populations: we established species compositions in each, compared sequencing and digital PCR method data, and investigated the robustness of digital PCR assays in quantifying H. contortus frequencies.
Most populations were dominated by H. contortus, with two harboring higher frequencies of T. circumcinta and Trichostrongylus. Sequencing-mediated species assignment failed to classify, on average, 21.3 % of the reads. Upon their removal, the concordance between the two evaluated methods was improved. Two populations containing low frequencies of H. contortus clustered separately from others and contained multiple high-frequency non-reference alleles in the H. contortus-specific probe-binding locus. Three or more of these were commonly present on the same DNA molecules. For one of the populations, we found signs of reduced amplification efficiency. Despite the genetic variation, the corresponding digital PCR data displayed sufficient cluster separation. Loci corresponding to universal primer and probe sequences, however, were well conserved. Ultimately, we showed that robust digital PCR assays can provide accurate species information on par with sequencing.
